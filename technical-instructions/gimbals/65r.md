# 65R

## 1.    Equipment and Materials

In addition to the items listed in the bill of materials, the following are also needed:

•       Loctite

•       Paper towel

•       65R Gimbal Screw Bin

## 2.    Initial Notes

1\. When fastening screws into the motors…

a.       Use a SMALL amount of Loctite. If too much is used, it will seep into the motor and cause jamming/stalling. See picture for correct amount of Loctite.

i. Dip into Loctite, the dab both the bottom and sides of the threads on a paper towel.

b.       First, fasten any screw and then fasten the one furthest from it. This helps to seat the motor evenly.

## 3.    Gimbal (21239-03) Assembly

1\.       Cut 4 zip ties off the snakeskin wire wrap protecting the motor wires. LEAVE THE SNAKESKIN ON

<p align="center"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (21).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

2\.       Attach arm to the motor between the snakeskin wire wraps as shown. Secure with 4 #8 screws and Loctite.

<p align="center"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (22).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

3\.       Attach cap and arm.

a.       Note: this step may be tricky to hold everything together at the same time. Consider using the 3D printed clamp.

b.       Route the wires as shown. Leave the frayed end of the snakeskin above the surface of the arm.

<p align="center"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (23).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

c.       Place the cap on over the screws secured in step 2.

<p align="center"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (24).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

d.       Hold second arm into place and feed shoulder #10 screws through the second arm, first arm, and into the cap. Secure with Loctite.

i. Note: it may be easiest to put one non-Loctite screw in to keep everything together while applying Loctite to the other screw.

<p align="right"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (25).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

4\.       Attach second motor to arm.

a.       Place second motor into arm and route the wires and make sure all the wires are passing THROUGH the hold and not being pinched.

b.       Insert 4 #8 screws with Loctite partially but do not tighten.

c.       Align the wires, snakeskin, and heat shrink and tighten screws.

<p align="right"><img src="file:///C:/Users/WTM5NBQ/AppData/Local/Temp/msohtmlclip1/01/clip_image015.gif" alt=""> </p>

d.       Check for any friction in the range of motion of the motor. If any exists, loosen screws and re-tighten.

5\.       Attach cover and cap.

a.       Pull the snakeskin close to the servo and squish with tweezers to ensure it will fit into the hole in the cover.

<p align="right"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (26).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

b.       Place the closed-end cover over the wire bundle and slide against the motor.

![](file:///C:/Users/WTM5NBQ/AppData/Local/Temp/msohtmlclip1/01/clip_image019.gif)&#x20;

c.       Place cap on the back side of the servo

d.       Secure 2 #9 screws and Loctite through the cover, arm and into the cap.

i. Before tightening completely, observe the snakeskin to ensure it is not under the cover but rather in the hole. Adjust as necessary.

<p align="right"><img src="file:///C:/Users/WTM5NBQ/AppData/Local/Temp/msohtmlclip1/01/clip_image022.gif" alt=""> </p>

e. Check for any friction by turning the motor and feeling for any tough spots.

&#x20;

&#x20;

6\.       Attach inter-most arm to motor.

a.       Feed the wires through the hole in the arm starting from the plug with the most wires to the least.

b.       Ensure the tab on the arm lines up in the space provided by the arm attached to the back of the motor. This allows the arm to be pointing inwards or upwards.

<p align="right"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (27).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

c.       Attach the arm to the motor using 3 #8 screws and Loctite.

<p align="center"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (28).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

d.       Check for any friction by turning the motor and feeling for any tough spots. If this occurs, loosen and re-tighten the screws.

7\.       Attach fourth arm to motor.

a.       Ensure the wires’ resting point is upwards by over-rotating the motor until the wires spring back to center (up).

<p align="center"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (29).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

b.       Place the arm on the motor such that the arm is pointing upward and the tab lined up in the space provided. Additionally, the heat shrink and wires should be between the two holes in the arm so they will not be pinched.

<p align="right"><img src="file:///C:/Users/WTM5NBQ/AppData/Local/Temp/msohtmlclip1/01/clip_image031.gif" alt=""> </p>

c.       Attach arm to the back of the motor using 4 #8 screws and Loctite.

<p align="right"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (30).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

d.       Check for any friction by turning the motor and feeling for any tough spots. If this occurs, loosen and re-tighten the screws.

8\.       Attach the open-end cover and cap.

a.       Ensure no wires are being pinched by squeezing the group of wires with tweezers.

b.       Place the open-end cover into place over the snakeskin and slide it against the motor.

![](<../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (31).jpeg>)&#x20;

c.       Place cap on the back of the motor.

<p align="center"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (32).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

d.       Secure 2 #9 screws and Loctite through the cover, arm and into the cap.

i. Before tightening completely, observe the snakeskin to ensure it is not under the cover but rather in the hole. Adjust as necessary.

<p align="right"><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (33).jpeg" alt=""> </p>

9\.       Check the entire gimbal for movement freedom by turning the arms and checking for any points of friction.

a.       If any friction occurs. Refer to fault isolation manual.
