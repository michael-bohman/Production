# Skyport Top Stack

## Required Materials



## Notes

This assembly is very close to a normal Skyport top stack with 2 exceptions:

1. Spacers in place of comms and motor controller boards.
2. Bend the top cap.

## Guide

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
### Modify The Yaw Cable Cover

Use a vise to bend carefully bend the tab on the yaw cable cover so that it is flat.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7680.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Attach The Yaw Adapter

1. Remove the two Torx T5 screws on the top of the Skyport.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7689.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

2. Remove the plate and two rubber pieces.
3. Line up the yaw adapter as shown. Secure with the 4 screws already in place.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7690.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

4. Replace the rubber pieces (nub side up) and top plate. Secure using the same Torx T5 screws from before.

TIP: it is best practice to screw the cap back on for the rest of the assembly to avoid scratching any part of the connector or letting debris in.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Plug In The PSDK Cable

Carefully plug in the PSDK cable. It may be easiest to hold it in place with your finger and use a larger hex driver (\~3mm) to apply pressure on different parts to secure it evenly.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7696.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Assemble The Rest Of The Stack

1. Place the comms board spacer on the yaw adapter where the comms board would normally go. Line it up using the USB-C notch and the 4 holes.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7697.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

2. Thread the cable through the large hole in the front of the yaw enclosure. Line it up on top of the spacer, being careful to keep the holes lined up.
3. Secure the yaw enclosure using 4 screws (94017A108) and Loctite.
   1. Note: Due to the greater load induced by the PixelScout system compared to a normal camera, apply a liberal amount of Loctite and tighten slightly more than usual.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7698.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

4. Place the motor controller spacer where the motor controller board would normally go. Line up the 3 screw holes.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7700.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

5. Line up the top of the gimbal using the 3 screw holes. Secure using 3 screws (94017A156) and Loctite.
   1. Note: it may be easiest to place toothpick halves into the screw holes to ensure things stay lined up until the screws are inserted.
   2. Note: Due to the greater load induced by the PixelScout system compared to a normal camera, apply a liberal amount of Loctite and tighten slightly more than usual.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7701.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

6. Place the yaw cable cover over the stack by inserting the tab into the slot. Secure using 2 screws (95836A512) and Loctite.

<figure><img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/IMG_7702.jpg" alt="" width="375"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}
